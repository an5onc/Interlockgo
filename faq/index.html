Colorado Program Requirements

Who is required to install an ignition interlock device in Colorado?

Colorado DMV requirements depend on your case, violation history, and reinstatement eligibility. Your current DMV reinstatement letter controls whether an interlock is required and which steps you must complete. If the letter is unclear, contact the DMV before scheduling.

How long do I need to keep an ignition interlock device?

The Colorado DMV determines your interlock period and any early-reinstatement eligibility. Dates can depend on the violation date, driving record, chemical-test history, and other restraints. Follow the dates and conditions in your current DMV reinstatement letter, and contact the DMV if anything is unclear.

What does the interlock program cost in Colorado?

Costs depend on the device, vehicle, service interval, taxes, optional protection, and current provider rate sheet. Use our next-visit estimator for a preview, then call 970‑515‑5740 to confirm current charges. DMV, insurance, education, or violation-related costs are separate.

What can cause the device to reject a breath sample?

Alcohol on your breath can cause the device to reject a sample. Some products containing alcohol may also affect a reading. Device settings and program rules vary, so follow the handset instructions, avoid driving after any alcohol use, and contact your provider if you receive an unexpected result. The Colorado DMV determines how reported events affect your case.

What is an SR‑22 and do I need one?

An SR‑22 is a certificate of financial responsibility filed by your insurance company with the Colorado DMV. It is not a type of insurance. Your reinstatement letter will say whether one is required. If it is, ask your insurer how it will be filed and keep the required coverage active until the DMV confirms the requirement has ended; a lapse may affect your license status.

Can I drive a vehicle without an interlock while enrolled in the program?

Follow the restriction printed on your license and in your DMV letter. In general, an interlock-restricted driver may operate only a vehicle equipped with the required functioning device. Ask the Colorado DMV before driving any vehicle that is not equipped.

Is an ignition interlock device the same as a breathalyzer?

Yes, people often use the word breathalyzer when they mean an ignition interlock device. The device is connected to your vehicle and requires a breath sample before start-up and during requested rolling retests.

Can InterlockGo help with Colorado DMV interlock paperwork?

Yes. We help clients understand the Colorado DMV interlock steps, installation reporting, affidavits, and reinstatement forms. We are not a law firm and do not provide legal advice, but we can help you prepare the interlock documents connected to your appointment.

How long does installation take?

Does the interlock work on any vehicle?

Ignition interlock devices can be installed on most gasoline and diesel passenger vehicles, trucks, SUVs, and motorcycles. Some older vehicles or those with unusual electrical systems may require additional work. Call us before your appointment if you have a diesel, hybrid, or electric vehicle so we can confirm compatibility.

Will installation damage my vehicle?

Our certified technicians use a professional, reversible installation process. When the device is removed, the technician restores the affected ignition wiring. Vehicle condition and electrical systems vary, so tell us about existing electrical issues before installation.

How does InterlockGo notify the Colorado DMV after installation?

We submit the provider records required after installation. Processing time can vary, so follow the sequence in your current DMV letter and confirm that the DMV has what it needs before relying on a license appointment date.

Daily Device Use

How do I start my vehicle with an ignition interlock?
  1. Get into your vehicle and put on your seatbelt.
  2. Do not put the key in the ignition yet.
  3. Press the button on the device and wait for "BLOW NOW" or the ready indicator.
  4. Blow a steady breath into the mouthpiece for 4–6 seconds as directed by the device.
  5. If you pass, the device will display "START" or "PASS" — insert your key and start the vehicle.
  6. If the device rejects the sample, follow the handset instructions. Do not try to start the vehicle until the device permits it.
What is a rolling retest and how do I handle it while driving?

A rolling retest is a breath sample requested after the vehicle is running. The handset will alert you when a test is due; timing and the allowed response window vary by device and program.

How to take it safely:

  1. When the device prompts you, acknowledge it and reduce speed.
  2. Pull over when it's safe to do so — you are not required to take the test while moving.
  3. Pick up the handset and blow a steady breath as directed.
  4. Wait for the device to confirm "PASS" before continuing to drive.

A missed or rejected retest may be recorded. Follow the handset instructions and contact your provider if you are unsure what to do.

What is the hum pattern and why do I need to do it?

The "blow and hum" is a technique built into some devices to prevent someone else from providing a breath sample on your behalf. The device detects the vibration of humming through the mouthpiece while you blow. When prompted to hum, blow steadily and hum a low note simultaneously. Keep continuous airflow — do not stop and restart. We demonstrate exactly how to do this during your post-installation training session.

I failed a test and I haven't been drinking — what do I do?

Products that contain alcohol can sometimes affect a reading, including:

  • Mouthwash or toothpaste containing alcohol
  • Hand sanitizer (alcohol vapors lingering in a closed vehicle)
  • Fermented beverages
  • Certain medications or breath sprays

What to do: Stop, follow the instructions displayed by your device, and do not drive unless the device permits it. Record the time and any products recently used, then contact your device provider if the result persists or you need guidance. Every attempt may be logged, and the Colorado DMV—not the service center—determines whether a reported event affects your case.

Does the interlock drain my car battery?

Interlock devices use a small amount of standby power. Battery condition, vehicle electronics, weather, and how often the vehicle is driven can all affect battery performance. LifeSafer L250 and FC100 requirements may differ by installation. If your battery is weak or aging, have it tested before installation. If it dies with the device installed, contact us or the manufacturer before disconnecting or jump-starting anything so you can follow the correct procedure.

Can my passenger take the test for me?

No. The enrolled driver should provide every required breath sample. Devices may record images and event data, and suspected circumvention may be reported under provider and DMV rules. The DMV, court, or supervising authority determines any consequence.

Calibration & Service

How often do I need to come in for calibration?

Follow the service date assigned by your device provider and shown on your paperwork or handset. The required interval can depend on the provider and program. We schedule appointments in advance and send reminders; call before your deadline if you need to reschedule.

What happens if I miss my calibration appointment?

A device that passes its service deadline may enter lockout mode and may require provider assistance or service before normal operation resumes. Missed service can also be recorded and reported under your program rules. Call us as soon as possible if you may miss an appointment so we can explain the available options.

What happens during a calibration visit?

During your calibration visit our technician will:

  • Download recorded data and transmit it through the provider's required reporting process
  • Verify device accuracy and recalibrate as needed
  • Inspect all hardware connections
  • Review any logged events (fails, missed retests) with you
  • Schedule your next appointment before you leave

Most visits take 20–30 minutes.

Can I get calibrated at any LifeSafer or Guardian location?

Service at another authorized location may be possible, but it must be coordinated with your device provider. Call the provider before traveling or changing service centers; do not assume every location can access your account or meet your deadline.

Violations & Lockouts

What happens when the device records alcohol-related events?

Recorded events may be included in provider reports to the Colorado DMV. How the DMV evaluates a pattern of events depends on current rules and your individual record. Follow the instructions on the device, contact your provider if you receive an unexpected result, and use your reinstatement letter or the official DMV program page for case-specific requirements.

What is a lockout and how do I get a temporary override code?

A lockout occurs when the device prevents the vehicle from starting. Common causes include a missed calibration appointment, repeated failed tests, or a detected tamper event.

To get a temporary override code so you can drive to a service center:

  • LifeSafer: Call 1‑800‑SAFERGO or log in at myaccount.lifesafer.com
  • Guardian: Call 1‑800‑499‑0994

An override code may temporarily lift the lockout so you can reach a service center. It does not delete the underlying device record; that record is handled according to provider and DMV reporting rules.

Will failed tests and violations be reported to the DMV and my court?

Device records are downloaded and transmitted according to your provider's process and Colorado DMV program rules. The DMV or another supervising authority decides how reported events affect your case. Operate the device as trained and keep every required service appointment.

Can someone tamper with or bypass the device?

Do not tamper with, bypass, disconnect, or remove the device yourself. Devices can record power interruptions and other events, and suspected circumvention may be reported. License or legal consequences depend on the facts and the decision of the DMV or other authority. If the device or vehicle needs repair, contact your provider first for the approved procedure.

Removal & Reinstatement

How do I know when I'm eligible to have the device removed?

The date on your restricted license or earlier correspondence may not, by itself, authorize removal. Before scheduling removal:

  • Review your current DMV reinstatement letter and confirm that all listed requirements are complete.
  • Contact the Colorado DMV to confirm your eligibility.
  • Obtain your unrestricted license or written DMV confirmation that the device may be removed.
  • Confirm any additional authorization required by your device provider.

We can help explain the service steps, but only the DMV can confirm your driving eligibility. Do not remove or disconnect the device yourself.

What is the removal process?

Once the DMV has issued your unrestricted license or confirmed in writing that removal is allowed:

  1. Confirm any required release with your device provider.
  2. Call us to schedule a removal appointment.
  3. Bring your unrestricted license or DMV confirmation and any provider documents.
  4. Our technician removes the device and restores the affected ignition wiring.
  5. We complete the provider documentation associated with the removal.

Call us for current removal pricing.

Do I receive my unrestricted license before or after removal?

Contact the Colorado DMV and obtain your unrestricted license or written confirmation that removal is allowed before the device is removed. Your reinstatement letter and the DMV's current instructions control the sequence for your case. The service center cannot authorize removal or unrestricted driving.

What happens to my SR‑22 insurance after removal?

Your SR‑22 requirement may continue after the interlock is removed. Do not cancel or change the required coverage until you receive confirmation from the Colorado DMV that it is no longer needed. An early lapse may affect your license status, so confirm the end date with both your insurer and the DMV.
